# DelishAfrica® Courier — Fair Pay Co-Design Question Pack Belgium

Date: 27 October 2026
Audience: CSC United Freelancers / Maison des Livreurs / courier representatives
Status: INTERNAL MEETING SUPPORT — LISTENING FIRST

## Purpose

Use the Fair Pay Lab as a concrete discussion object without presenting a finished Belgian employment/payroll model.

The objective is to validate **principles and pain points before legal and operational lock-in**.

## Opening position

DelishAfrica® is testing a model where:

- courier pay entitlement and platform profitability are separate;
- a bad zone cannot reduce already earned courier pay;
- controlled waiting is visible instead of disappearing;
- required repositioning is visible;
- tips and expense reimbursements are separate from the pay floor;
- every calculation has a version and an audit trail;
- a courier can contest their own weekly statement;
- a human reviews material disputes;
- corrections create a new traceable statement instead of erasing history;
- dispatch refusal alone is not intended to lower future assignment ranking.

These are design commitments under test, **not a final Belgian contract or wage table**.

## 1. Status / Core Fleet

Current POG laboratory direction:
**Core Fleet employee architecture**.

Questions:
1. From your field experience, what are the biggest weaknesses of current employee, independent and hybrid models?
2. Which protections do couriers lose most often in practice?
3. Are there important courier profiles for whom an employee Core Fleet would be unattractive?
4. Which flexibility features are genuinely valued and should survive inside an employee model?
5. What would make a “secure but flexible” contract credible rather than marketing language?

## 2. Paid time

Current Fair Pay buckets:
- active mission;
- platform-controlled waiting;
- required reposition;
- other paid work;
- freely disposable/excluded time.

Questions:
1. Which waiting situations must never become unpaid?
2. How should restaurant waiting be evidenced fairly?
3. When should repositioning between demand zones count as paid work?
4. Are there necessary tasks before/after a mission that platforms routinely ignore?
5. What situations look like “free time” in an app but are not genuinely free for the courier?

## 3. Pay floor

Current LAB:
- legal/CBA floor: intentionally OPEN;
- POG fairness simulation: 19 EUR/paid hour;
- engine always selects the higher applicable worker entitlement.

Questions:
1. Beyond the legal minimum, what makes hourly pay predictable and fair?
2. Should a Fair Pay commitment be hourly, weekly, per-shift, or a combination?
3. Which bonuses should remain separate from the guaranteed floor?
4. Which expenses must never be absorbed by the courier?
5. Which costs are most underestimated today: bike, phone, data, repairs, weather gear, charging/fuel, insurance, parking?

Important:
**19 EUR is a simulation parameter, not a Belgian wage proposal.**

## 4. Waiting and merchant accountability

Questions:
1. At what waiting duration does restaurant delay become unacceptable?
2. Should the courier be paid from arrival, from a validated “ready” signal, or through another rule?
3. What evidence should settle a disagreement between Merchant and Courier timestamps?
4. Should chronically late merchants face operational consequences?
5. What information should a courier see before accepting a mission?

## 5. Scheduling / flexibility

Questions:
1. What minimum notice for shifts feels reasonable?
2. How important is last-minute availability?
3. Should couriers choose windows, fixed shifts, or both?
4. How should cancellations by the platform be compensated?
5. What happens if demand is lower than expected during a scheduled paid window?

POG design principle:
**demand forecasting error should not silently become unpaid courier time.**

## 6. Weekly statement / transparency

Current LAB statement separates:
- paid time;
- active mission;
- waiting;
- reposition;
- other paid work;
- eligible income;
- governing floor;
- Fair Pay top-up;
- tips;
- expense reimbursements;
- statement/policy/facts audit hashes.

Questions:
1. Which information is actually useful to a courier?
2. What is missing?
3. What is too technical?
4. Should the courier see mission-level detail or weekly aggregation by default?
5. Which downloadable format would be useful?

## 7. Dispute / representation

Current LAB:
- courier can contest own statement;
- reason: time / amount / other;
- human review required;
- no silent statement rewrite;
- resolution can state “correction required”;
- actual correction is append-only and produces a new statement.

Questions:
1. What response SLA would be credible?
2. Should a representative/union be allowed to assist the courier?
3. What evidence should the courier be able to upload?
4. What should happen while a dispute is unresolved?
5. Should the undisputed portion still be paid on schedule?
6. Which decisions require a second human review?

## 8. Algorithmic management

Current design direction:
- refusal alone does not reduce assignment ranking;
- no permanent deactivation based only on automated scoring;
- material account decisions should have human review;
- algorithm factors should be documented.

Questions:
1. Which dispatch behaviors currently feel punitive or opaque?
2. What factors should never affect future access to missions?
3. Which ranking information should be visible?
4. How should fraud/safety signals be handled without creating false-positive punishment?
5. What appeal rights should exist for algorithmic decisions?

## 9. Safety / equipment / incidents

Questions:
1. What is the minimum equipment DelishAfrica should provide or reimburse?
2. What safety equipment is most neglected today?
3. What emergency support is actually useful during a shift?
4. How should dangerous zones/events affect mission offers?
5. Should a safety cancellation preserve pay?
6. What weather thresholds should trigger operational adaptation?

## 10. Pilot design

Proposed principle:
small dense micro-zone, limited fleet, explicit measurement, no city-wide burn.

Questions:
1. What would make couriers willing to participate in a four-week pilot?
2. How many founding couriers are enough for meaningful feedback?
3. Which metrics should be shared with courier representatives?
4. How often should the group meet during the pilot?
5. What conditions should stop the pilot immediately?

## Desired outcome of consultation

Not:
“CSC approves DelishAfrica.”

Instead:
- list of unacceptable failure modes;
- list of must-have protections;
- priorities for paid time;
- transparency requirements;
- dispute/representation requirements;
- pilot participation conditions;
- legal/payroll questions to refer to qualified specialists.
